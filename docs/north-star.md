# Teletubby — North Star

## The guiding idea

**Put the words in front of the talent in a shape they can talk to rather than read from — and
learn from every fumbled take so the next one comes out better.**

## What this means in practice

- **"Talent" is the person on camera** — the industry word, and precise here because three roles
  that are usually one person will eventually split: whoever **originated** the idea, the
  **talent** who says it, and the **operator** driving the app. On an AppyDave video all three
  are David. On the Kybernesis explainers they already split — Tom originates, David is talent
  and operator — and that is a teammate, not a client. Split them again for a client and the
  word earns its keep a third time.
- **It is for David first**, on his journey as AppyDave to be an awesome YouTuber: information
  lands in front of his eyes fast, in the right shape, so he can talk to it on camera like a
  professional. Beyond him, the audience is **any content creator who struggles with reading a
  script** — and script reading is inherently a dynamic editing process, because you say
  something that doesn't work and start again.
- **Open source is the distribution; the community is the business.** David does not sell
  products, he sells membership. Anyone who wants this can take the open-source version; anyone
  who wants hand-holding joins the community. So other creators are a real audience — not
  hypothetical users to build settings for, and not people whose feature requests outrank the
  talent's.
- **Meaning belongs to provenance; voice belongs to the talent.** The original script or idea —
  written by a teammate, a client, or the talent themselves — is the *provenance*. What David
  produces from it is a spin-off expressing how he wants to **say** it. Teletubby may move the
  wording toward how he actually speaks. It may never change what the script means.
- **It learns from the mistakes.** Over time the system learns from what goes wrong during
  recording and improves both the transcripts and the teleprompter technique. **"Prompting" here
  always means teleprompter craft — never LLM prompt engineering.** The two words must not be
  allowed to blur.
- **Scripts are editable — but Teletubby does not edit them.** It exposes the tools that let an
  agent do the editing: read scripts, read projects, create, edit, write, update. That agent may
  live inside Teletubby or in Claude Code; both are peers, neither is privileged. This is vibe
  directing — the chat operates the app. **The answer is "yes, but only by making itself
  drivable"**, never by growing an editor. Human editing controls: probably not.
- **Teletubby sits before the recording chain and never touches a file.** The talent drives Ecamm
  himself; FliHub watches the folder, queues every take, and promotes the one he keeps.
- **The twelve Kybernesis scripts are the first customer, not the product.** This eventually
  serves Alex, David, vOz and everyone.

## What this is NOT

- **Not a script editor**, and not an authoring tool — it makes itself drivable instead
- **Not a recorder.** It never captures, never stitches, never owns a video file
- **Not an LLM-prompting tool.** Prompting means teleprompter, always
- **Not a product to be sold.** The open-source app is distribution for the community
- **Not a cockpit.** If the talent has to learn it to use it, that is the defect
- **Not a thing that guesses what column 2 should say.** Until takes settle it, triggers are
  authored by hand

## The test

**Does it put more of the talent's attention on the camera, and less on the screen?**

If it removes something to read, or gets the right words in front of them sooner, it fits. If it
adds something to read, or a control to learn, it does not.

## Memorable framing

> "Glance at it, don't read it. And when I fluff it, the tool should get smarter, not me."

## Open

1. **What a trigger word actually is.** Column 2 is the product and the rule for generating it is
   undefined. **The talent decides, by recording takes** — same script, different trigger styles,
   keep whichever one lets him stop glancing at the transcript. Ruled 2026-08-19 via the side
   channel; the answer itself is still unrecorded, and no take has ever been shot from the one
   surviving specimen. **Not to be settled by argument.**
2. **One provenance script, or many transcripts from it?** You do not know until you speak whether
   a better idea arrives. Open: several scripts hanging off one provenance script, or the
   provenance script duplicated with new directional controls. **David leans to the latter** — his
   lean, recorded as unresolved, not as settled.
3. **Where transcripts sit in the project taxonomy.** Video, image and design projects are three
   established types; a transcript today is an artifact *inside* a video project rather than a
   type of its own. Matters because Teletubby gets segmented for clients later.
4. **Voice vs verbal style is one document doing two jobs.** The prescriptive style rules and the
   authored stance beneath them live under one name. Recommended fix is a labelled section, not a
   second file — a second file would drift and break a live citation. Not yet ruled.
